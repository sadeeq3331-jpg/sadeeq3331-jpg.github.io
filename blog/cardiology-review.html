Cardiology Quick Review

Published: March 2026 · 7 min read

Cardiology is a core system for USMLE Step 1, Step 2 CK, and clinical rotations. Here’s a condensed review of high‑yield concepts, with links to deeper resources in MedLib.

1. Heart Sounds

  • S1 – closure of mitral and tricuspid valves (loudest at apex)
  • S2 – closure of aortic and pulmonic valves (splits with inspiration)
  • S3 – rapid ventricular filling (normal in children, pathologic in adults → heart failure)
  • S4 – atrial contraction (pathologic → stiff ventricle, hypertrophy)

2. Murmurs

  • Aortic stenosis – harsh systolic, crescendo‑decrescendo, radiates to carotids → syncope, angina, dyspnea
  • Aortic regurgitation – diastolic decrescendo, bounding pulses, widened pulse pressure
  • Mitral stenosis – diastolic, opening snap, left atrial enlargement
  • Mitral regurgitation – pansystolic, radiates to axilla
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy – systolic murmur that increases with Valsalva

3. Ischemic Heart Disease

STEMI vs. NSTEMI:

  • STEMI – ST elevation, transmural, emergent reperfusion (PCI or thrombolytics)
  • NSTEMI – ST depression or T inversion, subendocardial, treat with anticoagulation

Biomarkers: Troponin I (most specific), CK‑MB (rises earlier, useful for reinfarction)

💡 Clinical Pearl: Right ventricular infarction often accompanies inferior MI (ST elevation in II, III, aVF). Avoid nitrates; maintain preload with fluids.

4. Heart Failure

  • HFrEF (systolic) – reduced ejection fraction (<40%). Causes: ischemic, dilated cardiomyopathy, alcohol.
  • HFpEF (diastolic) – preserved ejection fraction (>50%). Causes: hypertension, aging, restrictive cardiomyopathy.

Treatment: ACE inhibitors/ARBs, beta‑blockers, diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin).

5. Arrhythmias

  • Atrial fibrillation – irregularly irregular, risk of stroke → anticoagulate with CHA₂DS₂‑VASc score
  • Ventricular tachycardia – wide QRS, unstable → immediate cardioversion
  • Supraventricular tachycardia (AVNRT) – narrow QRS, regular, treat with adenosine (AV node block)

6. Key Medications

  • Aspirin / Clopidogrel – antiplatelet, used in ACS and after stenting
  • Nitroglycerin – venodilator, relieves angina
  • Digoxin – increases contractility, narrow therapeutic window
  • Amiodarone – antiarrhythmic (class III), pulmonary and thyroid toxicity

7. ECG Patterns

  • STEMI localisation: V1‑V2 → septal, V3‑V4 → anterior, II/III/aVF → inferior, I/aVL → lateral
  • Wellens’ syndrome – biphasic or deep T waves in V2‑V3 → critical LAD stenosis
  • Long QT syndrome – risk of torsades de pointes (drugs, hypokalemia, congenital)
