Biochemistry Mnemonics

Published: March 2026 · 6 min read

Biochemistry can feel overwhelming, but mnemonics help you lock in pathways and facts. Here are some of the most useful ones for USMLE Step 1 and medical school exams.

1. Essential Amino Acids (PVT TIM HALL)

  • Phenylalanine
  • Valine
  • Threonine
  • Tryptophan
  • Isoleucine
  • Methionine
  • Histidine
  • Arginine
  • Leucine
  • Lysine

Alternatively: “These Ten Valuable Amino Acids Have Long Preserved Life In Man” – Threonine, Tryptophan, Valine, Arginine, Histidine, Leucine, Phenylalanine, Lysine, Isoleucine, Methionine.

2. Ketogenic vs. Glucogenic Amino Acids

“KITTEN” – Ketogenic amino acids: K – Lysine, I – Isoleucine, T – Tryptophan, T – Threonine, E – Leucine? Wait, let’s fix: Actually, Lysine and Leucine are purely ketogenic. The others are both. For pure ketogenic: “LL” (Lysine, Leucine).

Glucogenic + Ketogenic: “PITT” (Phenylalanine, Isoleucine, Tyrosine, Tryptophan).

3. Glycolysis Enzymes (The Mighty ATP)

“Hungry Peter Pan And The Growling Gorilla Made Pie”

  • Hexokinase (or Glucokinase)
  • Phosphoglucose isomerase
  • Phosphofructokinase-1 (rate‑limiting)
  • Aldolase
  • Triose phosphate isomerase
  • Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase
  • Glycerate kinase (3‑phosphoglycerate kinase)
  • Mutase (phosphoglycerate mutase)
  • Enolase
  • Pyruvate kinase

4. Krebs Cycle Intermediates (Citrate Is Krebs Starting Substrate For Making Oxaloacetate)

“Citrate Is Krebs Starting Substrate For Making Oxaloacetate”

  • Citrate
  • Isocitrate
  • α‑Ketoglutarate
  • Succinyl‑CoA
  • Succinate
  • Fumarate
  • Malate
  • Oxaloacetate

5. Glycogen Storage Diseases (Vowels)

  • Type I – von Gierke (glucose‑6‑phosphatase deficiency) → severe hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly
  • Type II – Pompe (α‑1,4‑glucosidase) → lysosomal, cardiomegaly
  • Type III – Cori (debranching enzyme) → mild hypoglycemia
  • Type IV – Anderson (branching enzyme) → cirrhosis, death
  • Type V – McArdle (muscle phosphorylase) → exercise intolerance

6. Coenzymes and Vitamins

  • B1 (Thiamine) – TPP in pyruvate dehydrogenase, α‑ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase → “T” for Thiamine, “T” for TPP
  • B2 (Riboflavin) – FAD, FMN → “R” for Riboflavin, “R” for Redox
  • B3 (Niacin) – NAD, NADP → “N” for Niacin, “N” for NAD
  • B6 (Pyridoxine) – PLP in transamination, decarboxylation → “P” for Pyridoxine, “P” for PLP
  • B12 (Cobalamin) – methylmalonyl‑CoA mutase → “C” for Cobalamin, “C” for CoA
