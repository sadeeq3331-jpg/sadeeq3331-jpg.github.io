1. Cholinergic Agonists

Classification & Key Drugs

ClassDrugsMechanism & Main Use
Direct (Muscarinic)Bethanechol, Pilocarpine, CarbacholBind directly to muscarinic receptors. Uses: Urinary retention (Bethanechol), Glaucoma/Xerostomia (Pilocarpine).
Indirect (Reversible AChE Inhibitors)Neostigmine, Pyridostigmine, Physostigmine, EdrophoniumBlock acetylcholinesterase. Uses: Myasthenia Gravis (Neostigmine/Pyridostigmine). Anticholinergic overdose (Physostigmine crosses BBB).
Indirect (Irreversible AChE Inhibitors)Organophosphates, EchothiophatePermanent phosphorylation of AChE. Uses: Insecticides (toxicity), glaucoma (Echothiophate).

Mnemonic: Cholinergic Toxidrome

SLUDGE + BBB (Bronchorrhea, Bradycardia, Bronchospasm):
Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Diaphoresis, GI cramps, Emesis.

Absolute Contraindications & Cautions

  • Asthma – causes bronchospasm.
  • Peptic Ulcer Disease – increases gastric acid.
  • GI/GU Obstruction – perforation risk.
  • Bradycardia – additive effect with beta-blockers.

Clinical Pearls

  • Organophosphate Poisoning: Treat with Atropine (blocks muscarinic effects) + Pralidoxime (reactivates AChE if given early).
  • Myasthenia Gravis Diagnosis: Edrophonium (short-acting; rapid transient improvement).
  • Anticholinergic Overdose: Physostigmine is the ONLY reversible AChE inhibitor that crosses the BBB.

Benztropine / TrihexyphenidylParkinson's tremor / Drug-induced EPS.

Mnemonic: Anticholinergic Toxicity

"Blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter, hot as a hare."
(Mydriasis, dry mouth, flushing, delirium, hyperthermia).

Contraindications

  • Closed-angle Glaucoma – increases intraocular pressure.
  • Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH) – urinary retention.
  • Severe Tachycardia or Obstructive GI disease (e.g., pyloric stenosis).
3. Adrenergic Agonists

Receptor Selectivity & Clinical Uses

ReceptorDrug ExamplesClinical Use
α1Phenylephrine, MidodrineNasal decongestant, Orthostatic hypotension.
α2Clonidine, DexmedetomidineHypertension, Sedation (central sympatholytic).
β1DobutamineHeart failure (inotrope).
β2Albuterol, TerbutalineAsthma (bronchodilation), Tocolytic (delays labor).
α1 + β1 + β2Epinephrine, Norepinephrine, DopamineAnaphylaxis, Cardiac arrest, Shock.

Clinical Pearls

  • Epinephrine is first-line for anaphylaxis (IM into anterolateral thigh).
  • Dopamine at low doses acts on D1 receptors causing renal vasodilation (historically "renal dose", though efficacy debated).

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to sulfites (often in epinephrine preparations).
  • Uncontrolled tachyarrhythmias or Ventricular fibrillation (except during CPR).
  • Digitalis toxicity (epinephrine can worsen arrhythmias).
4. Alpha‑Adrenergic Antagonists

Selective vs Non-Selective

DrugSelectivityMain Uses
PhenoxybenzamineNon-selective (irreversible)Pheochromocytoma (pre-operative).
PhentolamineNon-selective (reversible)Hypertensive crisis (due to catecholamine excess).
Prazosin / DoxazosinSelective α1Hypertension, BPH.
TamsulosinSelective α1A (uroselective)BPH only (minimal BP effect).

Contraindications & Cautions

  • Orthostatic hypotension (especially first-dose syncope – warn patients to take at bedtime).
  • Severe cardiac failure or mitral stenosis.
  • Concomitant use with PDE5 inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil) – severe hypotension risk.
5. Beta‑Adrenergic Antagonists

Classification & Properties

DrugCardioselective (β1)ISAAlpha-blockade
Propranolol, Nadolol, TimololNo (β1 + β2)NoNo
Metoprolol, Atenolol, BisoprololYesNoNo
Pindolol, AcebutololYes (Pindolol non-select)Yes (ISA)No

Uses: Hypertension, Angina, Heart Failure (Carvedilol/Bisoprolol), Arrhythmias, Migraine prophylaxis, Glaucoma (Timolol).

Absolute Contraindications

  • Acute bronchial asthma (non-selective β-blockers block β2 bronchodilation).
  • Sinus bradycardia & Heart block (2nd/3rd degree).
  • Decompensated heart failure (acute).
  • Diabetes – non-selective β-blockers mask hypoglycemic symptoms (tachycardia).

9. Anticoagulants & Antiplatelets

Anticoagulants (Target Coagulation Cascade)

DrugMechanismUseReversal Agent
Unfractionated HeparinBinds Antithrombin III → Inactivates IIa & XaAcute DVT/PE, ACSProtamine Sulfate
LMWH (Enoxaparin)Inactivates Xa > IIaProphylaxis & treatmentProtamine (partial)
WarfarinInhibits Vit. K-dependent factors (II, VII, IX, X)Chronic Afib, Mechanical valvesVit. K + FFP
DOACs (Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban, Apixaban)Dabigatran: IIa inhib. Rivaroxaban/Apixaban: Xa inhib.Afib, DVT/PE (preferred)Idarucizumab (Dabigatran), Andexanet alfa (Xa inhib.)

Antiplatelet Agents

  • Aspirin: Irreversible COX-1 inhibitor. Use: Secondary MI/stroke. CI: Allergy, GI bleed, Reye's syndrome.
  • Clopidogrel / Prasugrel: P2Y12 (ADP) antagonist. Use: ACS, stent thrombosis.
  • Abciximab / Eptifibatide: GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors. Use: During PCI.

Mnemonic: Heparin vs Warfarin

"H" for Heparin = Hospital (IV/SC, acute, Protamine)
"W" for Warfarin = Ward / Outpatient (Oral, chronic, Vit K).
Heparin requires aPTT monitoring; Warfarin requires PT/INR. Heparin is safe in pregnancy; Warfarin is TERATOGENIC.

Contraindications

  • Heparin: Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT), active major bleeding.
  • Warfarin: Pregnancy (Category X), active bleeding, uncontrolled HTN.
  • DOACs: Prosthetic mechanical heart valves, severe renal impairment (Dabigatran).
