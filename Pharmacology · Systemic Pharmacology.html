0. Pharmacokinetics & Pharmacodynamics
🧠 Simple Analogy: Imagine a bathtub. The tap is the dose you give, and the drain is how the body clears the drug. The time it takes for half the water to drain is called the Half-life. It takes 5 half-lives for the water level to stabilize (Steady State). For a drug like Digoxin (half-life = 36 hours), it takes almost a week to reach full effect!
  • Half-life (t½): Time for drug concentration to drop by 50%. Example: Penicillin (1 hr) vs. Amiodarone (58 days).
  • Steady State: Achieved after 4–5 half-lives. This is why warfarin takes 3-5 days to reach full therapeutic effect.
  • Loading Dose: "Big first dose" to get levels high fast. Formula: Loading Dose = (Volume of Distribution × Target Concentration) / Bioavailability. Used for Digoxin and Amiodarone.
  • Therapeutic Index (TI): The gap between an effective dose and a toxic dose. Drugs with a narrow TI (Warfarin, Digoxin, Phenytoin) require blood level monitoring to avoid severe side effects.
  • Volume of Distribution (Vd): A fancy way of saying where the drug goes. Low Vd (e.g., Heparin) = stays in the blood. High Vd (e.g., Diazepam) = goes into fat and tissues.

🧠 Mnemonic: "5 is the Magic Number"

It takes 5 half-lives to reach steady state and 5 half-lives to fully eliminate a drug after stopping it.

1. Cholinergic Agonists
  • Direct-Acting Muscarinic Agonists
    • Bethanechol: Selective M₂/M₃ agonist. Uses: Post-op urinary retention, neurogenic bladder. AE: Salivation, sweating, GI cramping. 🌟
    • Pilocarpine: Non-selective muscarinic agonist. Uses: Glaucoma, xerostomia. AE: Blurred vision, brow ache. 🌟
    • Carbachol: Muscarinic + nicotinic agonist. Uses: Glaucoma (refractory).
  • Indirect-Acting (Anticholinesterases)
    • Neostigmine: Reversible. Uses: Myasthenia, post-op ileus, reversal of NMBA. 🌟
    • Pyridostigmine: Long-acting. Uses: Chronic myasthenia.
    • Physostigmine: Crosses BBB. Uses: Anticholinergic overdose. 🌟
    • Edrophonium: Ultra-short. Uses: Diagnose myasthenia (Tensilon test). 🌟
    • Organophosphates: Irreversible. Uses: Insecticides. AE: SLUDGE + nicotinic. Antidote: Atropine + pralidoxime. 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: SLUDGE

    Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Diaphoresis, GI motility, Emesis — muscarinic excess.

2. Anticholinergic Drugs
  • Muscarinic Antagonists
    • Atropine: Non-selective. Uses: Bradycardia, pre-anesthetic, organophosphate antidote. 🌟
    • Scopolamine: Crosses BBB. Uses: Motion sickness. 🌟
    • Ipratropium / Tiotropium: Quaternary → no CNS. Uses: COPD, asthma.
    • Darifenacin / Solifenacin: M₃-selective. Uses: Overactive bladder.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter, hot as a hare"

    Atropine toxicity: mydriasis, dry mouth, flushed skin, delirium, hyperthermia.

3. Adrenergic Agonists
  • Alpha-1 Agonists: Phenylephrine, Midodrine.
  • Alpha-2 Agonists: Clonidine, Dexmedetomidine. 🌟
  • Beta-2 Agonists: Albuterol (SABA), Salmeterol (LABA), Terbutaline (tocolytic). 🌟
  • Non-Selective Beta: Isoproterenol.
  • Mixed α+β: Epinephrine (anaphylaxis), Norepinephrine (shock), Dopamine. 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Epinephrine — the 4‑in‑1 drug"

    α₁ (vasoconstriction), α₂, β₁ (↑ HR), β₂ (bronchodilation). First-line for anaphylaxis.

4. Alpha‑Adrenergic Antagonists
  • Non-Selective: Phenoxybenzamine (irreversible, pheochromocytoma), Phentolamine.
  • Selective α₁: Prazosin, Doxazosin, Terazosin (HTN, BPH), Tamsulosin (BPH only). 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Tamsulosin — for the prostate"

    Selective α₁A → relaxes bladder neck. Less orthostatic hypotension.

5. Beta‑Adrenergic Antagonists
  • Non-Selective: Propranolol, Nadolol, Timolol. 🌟
  • Cardioselective (β₁): Metoprolol, Atenolol, Bisoprolol, Esmolol. 🌟
  • With ISA: Pindolol, Acebutolol.
  • With α-blockade: Carvedilol, Labetalol. 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Beta‑blockers in heart failure"

    Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol — the "3 B's" for HFrEF.

6. Antiarrhythmic Drugs (Vaughan Williams)
  • Class I (Na⁺ blockers): Ia (Quinidine, Procainamide), Ib (Lidocaine, Mexiletine), Ic (Flecainide, Propafenone). 🌟
  • Class II (β-blockers): Propranolol, Metoprolol. 🌟
  • Class III (K⁺ blockers): Amiodarone, Sotalol, Dofetilide. 🌟
  • Class IV (Ca²⁺ blockers): Verapamil, Diltiazem. 🌟
  • Others: Adenosine, Digoxin. 🌟
  • ⚠️ Amiodarone: Requires baseline and annual Thyroid, Liver, and Pulmonary function tests due to risk of pulmonary fibrosis and thyroid dysfunction.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "ABCD"

    Amiodarone (Class III) — pulmonary fibrosis, thyroid, corneal deposits.
    Beta-blockers (Class II) — rate control.
    Calcium blockers (Class IV) — AV node.
    Digoxin — + inotrope, vagotonic.

7. Antihypertensive Drugs
  • ACE inhibitors: Captopril, Enalapril, Lisinopril. AE: Cough, angioedema, hyperkalemia. 🌟
  • ARBs: Losartan, Valsartan. No cough. 🌟
  • CCBs: DHP (Amlodipine) — vasodilation; Non-DHP (Verapamil) — negative chronotropy. 🌟
  • Thiazides: HCTZ, Chlorthalidone. 🌟
  • Beta-blockers.
  • 🧠 Why does ACE inhibitor cause a cough? ACE inhibitors block the breakdown of bradykinin, which accumulates in the lungs and causes a dry cough. Switch to an ARB if intolerant.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "ACE inhibitor cough"

    Bradykinin accumulation → dry cough. Switch to ARB if intolerant.

8. Diuretics
  • Loop: Furosemide, Bumetanide. MOA: Inhibit NKCC2. AE: Hypokalemia, ototoxicity. 🌟
  • Thiazides: HCTZ, Chlorthalidone. MOA: Inhibit Na⁺-Cl⁻ cotransporter. AE: Hypercalcemia, hyperuricemia. 🌟
  • K⁺-sparing: Spironolactone (aldosterone antagonist), Amiloride. 🌟
  • CA inhibitors: Acetazolamide. Uses: Glaucoma, altitude sickness.
  • Osmotic: Mannitol. Uses: ↓ ICP, ↓ IOP. 🌟
  • 🧠 How do Loops work? The thick ascending limb of the kidney is where we reabsorb salt. Loop diuretics block the "Na-K-2Cl" pump, so the salt stays in the urine and pulls water out with it.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Loops lose K⁺, Thiazides spare Ca²⁺"

    Loop → hypokalemia, hypocalcemia. Thiazide → hypercalcemia.

9. Anticoagulants & Antiplatelets
  • Anticoagulants: Heparin (IV/SC) — monitor aPTT, reverse with protamine. LMWH (Enoxaparin) — monitor anti-Xa. Warfarin — monitor INR, reverse with vitamin K. DOACs (Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban, Apixaban). 🌟
  • Antiplatelets: Aspirin (COX-1), Clopidogrel (P2Y12), Abciximab (GP IIb/IIIa). 🌟
  • Renal dosing: Enoxaparin dose adjustment required if CrCl < 30 mL/min. Heparin is not renally cleared.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Heparin vs. Warfarin"

    Heparin: Hospital (IV), HIT risk, Half-life short, Heparin antidote = protamine.
    Warfarin: Wait 2–3 days for effect, Watch INR, Warfarin antidote = vitamin K.

10. Lipid‑Lowering Agents
  • Statins: Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin. MOA: HMG-CoA reductase inhibition. AE: Myalgias, rhabdomyolysis. 🌟
  • Ezetimibe: ↓ cholesterol absorption.
  • PCSK9 inhibitors: Evolocumab, Alirocumab.
  • Fibrates: Fenofibrate — ↓ TG, ↑ HDL.
  • Niacin: ↓ VLDL, ↑ HDL. AE: Flushing (pretreat with aspirin). 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Statins — myalgia and CK"

    If myalgias occur, check CK. Risk of rhabdomyolysis ↑ with fibrates (especially gemfibrozil).

11. Drugs for Asthma & COPD
  • Bronchodilators: SABA (Albuterol), LABA (Salmeterol), LAMA (Tiotropium). 🌟
  • Anti-inflammatory: ICS (Fluticasone), LTRA (Montelukast), Cromolyn, Omalizumab (anti-IgE). 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Asthma step therapy"

    Step 1: SABA prn. Step 2: ICS. Step 3: ICS + LABA. Step 4: ICS + LABA + LAMA.

12. Drugs for Acid‑Peptic Disorders
  • Antacids: Aluminum/Mg hydroxide.
  • H₂ blockers: Famotidine, Ranitidine (withdrawn).
  • PPIs: Omeprazole, Esomeprazole — inhibit H⁺/K⁺ ATPase. 🌟
  • Sucralfate: Coats ulcers.
  • Misoprostol: Prevents NSAID ulcers. Contraindicated in pregnancy. 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "PPI — take before meals"

    PPIs are most effective when taken 30–60 min before breakfast.

13. Antiemetics
  • 5-HT₃: Ondansetron, Granisetron — CINV. 🌟
  • Dopamine antagonists: Metoclopramide (EPS risk), Prochlorperazine. 🌟
  • NK₁: Aprepitant — delayed CINV.
  • Antihistamines: Promethazine — motion sickness.
  • Anticholinergics: Scopolamine — motion sickness.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "CINV triple therapy"

    5-HT₃ + Dexamethasone + NK₁ antagonist for highly emetogenic chemotherapy.

14. Antidiabetic Drugs
  • Insulin: Rapid (Lispro), Short (Regular), Intermediate (NPH), Long (Glargine). 🌟
  • Biguanides: Metformin — first-line. AE: Lactic acidosis (rare). 🌟
  • Sulfonylureas: Glipizide — ↑ insulin. AE: Hypoglycemia. 🌟
  • TZDs: Pioglitazone — ↑ insulin sensitivity. AE: Fluid retention, fractures.
  • DPP-4 inhibitors: Sitagliptin.
  • GLP-1 agonists: Liraglutide, Semaglutide — weight loss, CV benefit. 🌟
  • SGLT2 inhibitors: Empagliflozin — glycosuria, CV/renal benefit. 🌟
  • ⚠️ Metformin: Contraindicated if eGFR < 30 mL/min due to the risk of life-threatening lactic acidosis.
  • Metformin: eGFR 30–45: use with caution; eGFR <30: do NOT use.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Metformin — first-line"

    Metformin is first-line for type 2 diabetes unless contraindicated (eGFR <30).

15. Thyroid & Anti‑thyroid Drugs
  • Replacement: Levothyroxine (T₄) — first-line. 🌟
  • Anti-thyroid: Methimazole (first-line for Graves'), PTU (thyroid storm, first trimester). 🌟
  • Radioiodine: ¹³¹I — definitive for Graves'.
  • Beta-blockers: Propranolol — controls symptoms.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Methimazole vs. PTU"

    Methimazole — once daily, less hepatotoxic. PTU — preferred in Pregnancy (1st trimester) and Pyroid storm.

16. Corticosteroids
  • Glucocorticoids: Prednisone, Methylprednisolone, Dexamethasone. Uses: Anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive. 🌟
  • Mineralocorticoid: Fludrocortisone — adrenal insufficiency.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Dexamethasone — no mineralocorticoid"

    Dexamethasone has zero mineralocorticoid activity — used in cerebral edema and COVID-19.

17. Sex Hormones & Contraceptives
  • Estrogens: Conjugated estrogens, Ethinyl estradiol.
  • Progestins: Progesterone, Levonorgestrel.
  • Combined OCs: Estrogen + progestin. Contraindications: Smoking >35, DVT/PE.
  • Androgens: Testosterone.
  • Anti-androgens: Spironolactone, Finasteride (5α-reductase inhibitor).
18. Sedative‑Hypnotics
  • Benzodiazepines: Diazepam, Lorazepam, Alprazolam. MOA: ↑ GABAₐ frequency. Antidote: Flumazenil. 🌟
  • Z-drugs: Zolpidem, Eszopiclone.
  • Barbiturates: Phenobarbital. AE: Enzyme induction, porphyria. 🌟
  • Melatonin agonists: Ramelteon.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "BZD antidote — Flumazenil"

    Flumazenil is a competitive BZD antagonist. Use cautiously in BZD-dependent patients (seizure risk).

19. Antidepressants
  • SSRIs: Fluoxetine, Sertraline — first-line. 🌟
  • SNRIs: Venlafaxine, Duloxetine. 🌟
  • TCAs: Amitriptyline — cardiotoxic in overdose. 🌟
  • MAOIs: Phenelzine — tyramine restrictions. 🌟
  • Atypicals: Bupropion (NDRI), Mirtazapine (NaSSA). 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "SSRI side effects"

    Sexual dysfunction, GI upset, insomnia/sedation, serotonin syndrome (with MAOIs).

20. Antipsychotics
  • First-generation: Haloperidol (D₂ antagonist) — EPS, tardive dyskinesia. 🌟
  • Second-generation: Risperidone, Olanzapine, Quetiapine, Aripiprazole (partial agonist), Clozapine (refractory — agranulocytosis risk). 🌟
  • ⚠️ Clozapine: Requires weekly WBC monitoring for the first 6 months due to the high risk of agranulocytosis. Use only for treatment-resistant schizophrenia.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Clozapine — agranulocytosis"

    Clozapine requires WBC monitoring. Use only for treatment-resistant schizophrenia.

21. Antiepileptic Drugs
  • Na⁺ blockers: Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Lamotrigine. 🌟
  • GABA enhancers: BZDs, Barbiturates, Tiagabine.
  • T-type Ca²⁺ blockers: Ethosuximide — absence seizures. 🌟
  • Multi-mechanism: Valproate, Levetiracetam, Topiramate. 🌟
  • 🧠 Phenytoin's tricky math: Phenytoin follows Zero-order kinetics. This means a small increase in dose causes a huge increase in blood levels (unlike most drugs). Always monitor levels closely!

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Phenytoin — zero-order kinetics"

    Small dose increases → large serum level changes. Therapeutic range: 10–20 μg/mL.

22. Drugs for Parkinson's Disease
  • Levodopa + Carbidopa: Most effective. AE: Dyskinesias, on-off. 🌟
  • Dopamine agonists: Pramipexole, Ropinirole. 🌟
  • COMT inhibitors: Entacapone — reduces wearing-off.
  • MAO-B inhibitors: Selegiline, Rasagiline.
  • Anticholinergics: Benztropine — for tremor. 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Parkinson's drugs"

    Levodopa — most effective. Dopamine agonists — younger patients. Amantadine — dyskinesias. COMT inhibitors — wearing-off.

23. Antibiotics
  • Penicillins:
    • Natural: Penicillin G — gram+ cocci, syphilis. AE: Hypersensitivity, seizures (high dose). 🌟
    • Aminopenicillins: Ampicillin, Amoxicillin — enterococci, H. influenzae.
    • Penicillinase-resistant: Nafcillin, Oxacillin — S. aureus (MSSA).
    • Antipseudomonal: Piperacillin — with β-lactamase inhibitor (Tazobactam).
  • Cephalosporins:
    • 1st gen: Cefazolin, Cephalexin — skin/soft tissue.
    • 2nd gen: Cefuroxime, Cefoxitin — B. fragilis (cefoxitin).
    • 3rd gen: Ceftriaxone, Cefotaxime — meningitis, gonorrhea. AE: Biliary sludge. 🌟
    • 4th gen: Cefepime — pseudomonas.
    • 5th gen: Ceftaroline — MRSA.
  • Carbapenems: Meropenem, Imipenem (with cilastatin) — broad-spectrum. AE: Seizures (imipenem). 🌟
  • Monobactams: Aztreonam — only gram‑negative. Safe in penicillin allergy.
  • Macrolides: Erythromycin, Azithromycin, Clarithromycin — atypical pneumonia. AE: GI upset, QT prolongation. 🌟
  • Aminoglycosides: Gentamicin, Amikacin — gram‑negative. AE: Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity. 🌟
  • Tetracyclines: Doxycycline, Minocycline — atypical, acne, Lyme disease. Contraindications: Pregnancy, children <8 (teeth discoloration). 🌟
  • Fluoroquinolones: Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin — UTI, respiratory. AE: Tendon rupture, QT prolongation, CNS effects. 🌟
  • Glycopeptides: Vancomycin — MRSA, C. difficile (oral). AE: Red man syndrome, nephrotoxicity. 🌟
  • Oxazolidinones: Linezolid — VRE, MRSA. AE: Myelosuppression, serotonin syndrome (with SSRIs). 🌟
  • Metronidazole: Anaerobes, C. difficile (oral). AE: Metallic taste, disulfiram-like reaction. 🌟
  • ⚠️ Tetracyclines: Absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy and children <8 years old because they cause permanent discoloration of developing teeth.
  • Vancomycin: Dose adjusted according to CrCl; target trough 15–20 mcg/mL for serious infections.
  • IV→PO conversion: Levofloxacin, Metronidazole, and Fluconazole have excellent oral bioavailability – same dose PO as IV.

24. Antimalarial Drugs
  • Chloroquine: MOA: Concentrates in parasite food vacuole → inhibits heme polymerization. Uses: P. vivax, P. falciparum (except resistant strains). AE: Pruritus, retinopathy (chronic use), QT prolongation. 🌟
  • Hydroxychloroquine: Less retinopathy. Uses: Malaria, SLE, rheumatoid arthritis.
  • Quinine / Quinidine: Uses: Severe P. falciparum (IV). AE: Cinchonism (tinnitus, headache, nausea), hypoglycemia, QT prolongation. 🌟
  • Mefloquine: Uses: Prophylaxis for chloroquine-resistant P. falciparum. AE: Neuropsychiatric (nightmares, psychosis, seizures). 🌟
  • Artemisinin derivatives: Artemether, Artesunate. MOA: Endoperoxide → free radicals. Uses: Severe malaria (first-line in many regions). AE: Neurotoxicity (rare), GI upset. 🌟
  • Atovaquone‑Proguanil (Malarone): Uses: Prophylaxis, treatment of P. falciparum. AE: GI upset, headache.
  • Primaquine: MOA: Kills hypnozoites (liver stage). Uses: Radical cure of P. vivax and P. ovale. Contraindications: G6PD deficiency (hemolysis). 🌟
  • Doxycycline: Uses: Prophylaxis (chloroquine-resistant areas). AE: Photosensitivity, GI upset.
  • Pyrimethamine‑Sulfadoxine (Fansidar): MOA: Inhibit folate synthesis. Uses: Intermittent preventive therapy in pregnancy. AE: Stevens-Johnson syndrome.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Antimalarial drugs"

    Chloroquine — Color (retinopathy). Quinine — QT prolongation, cinchonism. Primaquine — Prevent relapse (G6PD check). Artemisinins — Active against severe malaria.

25. Antiviral Drugs
  • Anti‑Herpesviruses:
    • Acyclovir, Valacyclovir: MOA: Inhibit HSV DNA polymerase. Uses: HSV, VZV. AE: Nephrotoxicity (IV), GI upset. 🌟
    • Foscarnet: Uses: Acyclovir-resistant HSV, CMV. AE: Nephrotoxicity, electrolyte imbalances.
    • Ganciclovir, Valganciclovir: Uses: CMV (retinitis, transplant). AE: Myelosuppression. 🌟
  • Anti‑Influenza:
    • Oseltamivir, Zanamivir: Neuraminidase inhibitors. Uses: Influenza A & B (within 48h of symptoms). AE: GI upset, bronchospasm (zanamivir). 🌟
    • Amantadine, Rimantadine: M2 ion channel blockers. Uses: Influenza A only (now limited due to resistance). AE: CNS effects, seizures.
  • Anti‑HIV (Antiretrovirals):
    • NRTIs: Zidovudine, Tenofovir, Emtricitabine. AE: Bone marrow suppression (ZDV), nephrotoxicity (tenofovir). 🌟
    • NNRTIs: Efavirenz, Nevirapine. AE: Rash, hepatotoxicity, CNS effects (efavirenz). 🌟
    • Protease inhibitors: Ritonavir, Atazanavir. AE: GI upset, hyperlipidemia, insulin resistance. 🌟
    • Integrase inhibitors: Dolutegravir, Raltegravir. AE: Insomnia, headache.
    • CCR5 antagonist: Maraviroc. Uses: CCR5‑tropic HIV.
  • Anti‑Hepatitis:
    • HCV: Sofosbuvir (NS5B inhibitor), Ledipasvir, Daclatasvir. Uses: Chronic HCV — curative. 🌟
    • HBV: Tenofovir, Entecavir. Uses: Chronic HBV suppression.
  • Anti‑SARS‑CoV‑2:
    • Remdesivir: RNA polymerase inhibitor. Uses: COVID-19 (hospitalized).
    • Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir (Paxlovid): Protease inhibitor. Uses: Mild-to-moderate COVID-19 (high-risk). 🌟
    • Molnupiravir: Mutagenic nucleoside. Uses: COVID-19 (alternative).

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Acyclovir — HSV/VZV"

    Acyclovir works on HSV and VZV. Valacyclovir is a prodrug with better oral bioavailability.

26. Antifungal Drugs
  • Polyenes:
    • Amphotericin B: Binds ergosterol → pore formation. Uses: Systemic mycoses (candidiasis, aspergillosis). AE: Nephrotoxicity, infusion reactions (fever, chills), hypokalemia. 🌟
    • Nystatin: Topical/oral for candidiasis.
  • Azoles:
    • Fluconazole: Uses: Candidiasis, cryptococcal meningitis. AE: Hepatotoxicity, QT prolongation. 🌟
    • Itraconazole / Voriconazole: Uses: Aspergillosis (voriconazole first-line), histoplasmosis. AE: Hepatotoxicity, visual disturbances (voriconazole). 🌟
    • Ketoconazole: Uses: Topical for tinea, seborrheic dermatitis (systemic use limited due to hepatotoxicity).
  • Echinocandins: Caspofungin, Micafungin, Anidulafungin. MOA: Inhibit β‑glucan synthase. Uses: Invasive candidiasis, aspergillosis (salvage). AE: Rash, fever, GI upset. 🌟
  • Allylamines: Terbinafine. MOA: Inhibit squalene epoxidase. Uses: Dermatophytosis (tinea, onychomycosis). AE: GI upset, hepatotoxicity.
  • Pyrimidine analog: Flucytosine. MOA: Inhibits thymidylate synthase. Uses: Cryptococcal meningitis (with amphotericin). AE: Bone marrow suppression.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Azoles — hepatotoxicity"

    All azoles can cause liver injury. Voriconazole — visual disturbances. Fluconazole — QT prolongation.

27. Anthelmintics
  • Albendazole / Mebendazole: MOA: Inhibit microtubule polymerization. Uses: Intestinal nematodes (roundworms, hookworms, whipworms), neurocysticercosis (albendazole). AE: GI upset, hepatotoxicity, bone marrow suppression. 🌟
  • Diethylcarbamazine (DEC): Uses: Filariasis (Wuchereria bancrofti, Brugia malayi). AE: Mazzotti reaction (fever, rash, lymphadenopathy) — due to dead microfilariae. 🌟
  • Ivermectin: MOA: Binds glutamate-gated Cl⁻ channels. Uses: Strongyloidiasis, onchocerciasis (river blindness), scabies, head lice. AE: Pruritus, rash, dizziness. 🌟
  • Praziquantel: MOA: Increases Ca²⁺ permeability → muscle spasm. Uses: Schistosomiasis, neurocysticercosis (with steroids), tapeworms. AE: Dizziness, abdominal pain. 🌟
  • Nicolasamide: Uses: Tapeworms (Taenia saginata, Diphyllobothrium).
  • Metronidazole: Uses: Giardiasis, amebiasis.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Anthelmintics"

    Albendazole — Ascaris, hookworm. Ivermectin — Infection (onchocerciasis). Praziquantel — Platyhelminthes (schistosomes, tapeworms).

28. NSAIDs & Acetaminophen
  • NSAIDs:
    • Non-selective COX inhibitors: Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Indomethacin, Ketorolac. MOA: Inhibit COX-1 & COX-2 → ↓ prostaglandins. Uses: Pain, inflammation, fever, dysmenorrhea. AE: GI ulcers (COX-1), renal impairment, bleeding risk, cardiovascular events (COX-2). 🌟
    • Selective COX-2 inhibitors: Celecoxib. Uses: Arthritis (less GI toxicity). AE: ↑ CV risk (MI, stroke). 🌟
    • High‑risk patients: Avoid NSAIDs in peptic ulcer disease, CKD, heart failure, bleeding disorders.
  • Acetaminophen (Paracetamol):
    • MOA: Central COX inhibition (weak peripheral). Uses: Pain, fever. AE: Hepatotoxicity (high doses — deplete glutathione). Antidote: N‑acetylcysteine. 🌟
    • Safe in pregnancy (category B), does not cause GI ulcers or bleeding.
  • NSAIDs: Avoid if eGFR < 30 mL/min; can cause acute kidney injury via afferent arteriole vasoconstriction.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "NSAID side effects"

    GI bleeding, Renal impairment, Cardiovascular risk — the "G.R.C." triad.

29. Opioid Analgesics
  • Full Agonists (μ): Morphine, Fentanyl, Hydromorphone, Oxycodone, Methadone. MOA: Activate μ-opioid receptors → ↑ K⁺ efflux, ↓ Ca²⁺ influx → hyperpolarization. Uses: Moderate-severe pain. AE: Respiratory depression, constipation, nausea, tolerance, dependence, miosis. 🌟
  • Partial Agonists: Buprenorphine (μ partial, κ antagonist). Uses: Opioid use disorder, pain. AE: Less respiratory depression.
  • Mixed Agonist‑Antagonists: Nalbuphine (κ agonist, μ antagonist), Pentazocine. Uses: Pain (limited). AE: Psychotomimetic effects.
  • Antagonists: Naloxone, Naltrexone. MOA: Competitive μ antagonist. Uses: Opioid overdose (naloxone), alcohol use disorder (naltrexone). 🌟
  • 🧠 Why do opioids cause constipation? Opioid receptors in the gut slow down peristalsis (muscle contractions) and increase fluid absorption. This is why we give stool softeners with chronic opioids!

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Opioid triad"

    Miosis, respiratory depression, coma — classic opioid overdose. Antidote: Naloxone.

30. Local Anesthetics
  • Esters: Procaine, Tetracaine, Benzocaine. Metabolism: Plasma cholinesterase. AE: Allergic reactions (metabolite PABA). 🌟
  • Amides: Lidocaine, Bupivacaine, Ropivacaine, Mepivacaine. Metabolism: Hepatic (CYP). AE: CNS toxicity (seizures), cardiotoxicity (bupivacaine). 🌟
  • MOA: Block voltage-gated Na⁺ channels → inhibit action potential conduction.
  • Addition of epinephrine: ↑ duration, ↓ systemic toxicity (except in end-artery areas — fingers, toes, penis).

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Esters vs. Amides"

    Esters = Extra allergic (PABA). Amides = Absorbed in liver, Allergies rare.

31. Immunosuppressants
  • Calcineurin inhibitors: Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus. MOA: Inhibit calcineurin → ↓ IL‑2 production → ↓ T‑cell activation. Uses: Organ transplant rejection, autoimmune diseases. AE: Nephrotoxicity, hypertension, hyperkalemia, gingival hyperplasia (cyclosporine), diabetes (tacrolimus). 🌟
  • mTOR inhibitors: Sirolimus, Everolimus. MOA: Inhibit mTOR → ↓ T‑cell proliferation. Uses: Transplant rejection (with calcineurin inhibitors). AE: Hyperlipidemia, myelosuppression, pulmonary toxicity.
  • Antimetabolites:
    • Mycophenolate mofetil: Inhibits IMPDH → ↓ guanine nucleotide synthesis → ↓ lymphocyte proliferation. Uses: Transplant rejection, autoimmune. AE: GI upset, myelosuppression, teratogenic.
    • Azathioprine: Purine analog. Uses: Transplant rejection, IBD, autoimmune. AE: Myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis. 🌟
    • Methotrexate: Dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor. Uses: Rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, cancer. AE: Hepatotoxicity, myelosuppression, pneumonitis. 🌟
  • Glucocorticoids: Prednisone — broad anti-inflammatory.
  • Monoclonal antibodies:
    • Basiliximab: Anti‑CD25 (IL‑2 receptor) — transplant.
    • Rituximab: Anti‑CD20 (B‑cell) — autoimmune, lymphoma. 🌟
    • Infliximab: Anti‑TNF‑α — IBD, RA, psoriasis.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Tacrolimus vs. Cyclosporine"

    Tacrolimus: Tremor, Transplant, Toxicity (nephro, diabetes).
    Cyclosporine: Cyclosporine = Calcineurin inhibitor, Causes Gingival hyperplasia.

33. Memory Aids & Mnemonics

🧠 Autonomic Nervous System

  • SLUDGE — Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Diaphoresis, GI motility, Emesis (muscarinic excess).
  • Atropine toxicity: "Blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter, hot as a hare."
  • β‑blockers in heart failure: Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol — the "3 B's".
  • Alpha‑1 blockers: "Prazosin, Doxazosin, Terazosin" — first-dose syncope (give at bedtime).

🧠 Cardiovascular

  • Antiarrhythmic "ABCD": A = Amiodarone (Class III), B = Beta-blockers, C = Calcium blockers, D = Digoxin.
  • Diuretics: "Loops lose K⁺, Thiazides spare Ca²⁺."
  • ACE inhibitor cough: Bradykinin accumulation. Switch to ARB.
  • Heparin vs. Warfarin: Heparin = Hospital (IV), HIT, short half-life. Warfarin = Wait (2‑3 days), Watch INR.

🧠 Anti‑infectives

  • Antibiotic coverage: Penicillins = gram+ cocci (except MRSA). Aminoglycosides = gram‑negatives. Vancomycin = MRSA. Metronidazole = anaerobes.
  • Cephalosporin generations: 1st = skin, 2nd = anaerobes (cefoxitin), 3rd = meningitis (ceftriaxone), 4th = pseudomonas (cefepime), 5th = MRSA (ceftaroline).
  • Antimalarial mnemonics: Chloroquine = retinopathy. Quinine = cinchonism. Primaquine = G6PD check. Artemisinin = severe malaria.
  • Azoles: All cause hepatotoxicity. Voriconazole = visual disturbances. Fluconazole = QT prolongation.

🧠 CNS & Pain

  • Opioid overdose triad: Miosis, respiratory depression, coma. Antidote: Naloxone.
  • Local anesthetics: Esters = allergic (PABA). Amides = hepatic metabolism, fewer allergies.
  • SSRI side effects: Sexual dysfunction, GI upset, insomnia/sedation, serotonin syndrome (with MAOIs).
  • Clozapine: Agranulocytosis — requires WBC monitoring. Reserved for refractory schizophrenia.

🧠 Endocrine & Immuno

  • Methimazole vs. PTU: Methimazole = once daily, less hepatotoxic. PTU = pregnancy (1st trimester) and thyroid storm.
  • Dexamethasone: No mineralocorticoid activity — used in cerebral edema and COVID-19.
  • Corticosteroid tapering: Never stop abruptly — adrenal crisis risk.
  • Calcineurin inhibitors: Cyclosporine = gingival hyperplasia. Tacrolimus = diabetes, tremor.

35. Gout & Muscle Relaxants

🦴 Gout (Uric Acid)

  • Acute Gout Attack:
    • Colchicine: Binds to tubulin, stops microtubule formation, and halts inflammation. Give early in an attack. AE: Diarrhea, GI upset. 🌟
    • NSAIDs: Indomethacin or Naproxen are first-line alternatives.
    • Corticosteroids: Oral or intra-articular if NSAIDs/colchicine are contraindicated.
  • Chronic Gout Prophylaxis:
    • Allopurinol: Xanthine oxidase inhibitor → ↓ uric acid production. Start low, titrate up. AE: Hypersensitivity, Stevens-Johnson syndrome. 🌟
    • Febuxostat: Alternative to allopurinol (non-purine analog).
    • Probenecid: Uricosuric agent → increases uric acid excretion. Contraindicated in kidney stones.
  • 🧠 Why do we give Colchicine with Allopurinol initially? When you lower uric acid, the crystals in the joints can shift and cause a "flare" (an acute attack). Colchicine prevents this inflammation while the allopurinol takes effect.

💪 Neuromuscular Blockers (Muscle Relaxants)

  • Depolarizing (Succinylcholine): Binds to nicotinic receptors, causes initial fasciculations (muscle twitches) then paralysis. Duration: Ultra-short (~5 minutes). Contraindications: Hyperkalemia, burns, muscular dystrophy (risk of massive K⁺ release). 🌟
  • Non-Depolarizing (Rocuronium, Vecuronium): Competitive antagonists at the nicotinic receptor. Duration: Intermediate. Reversal: Sugammadex (wraps around Rocuronium like a cup and removes it). 🌟

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Sugammadex – the Rocuronium remover"

    Sugammadex is a selective binding agent that completely reverses Rocuronium and Vecuronium without the side effects of older reversal agents (like neostigmine).

36. Cancer Chemotherapy
🧠 Simple Analogy: Chemotherapy drugs are like "weed killers" – they target fast-growing cells. The problem is, they also hit normal fast-growing cells: hair follicles, bone marrow, and the gut lining. This is why patients experience hair loss, low blood counts, and diarrhea.
  • 1. Alkylating Agents (DNA cross-linkers):
    • Cyclophosphamide: Requires activation in the liver. AE: Hemorrhagic cystitis (bladder bleeding). Give MESNA to protect the bladder. 🌟
    • Cisplatin: Platinum-based. AE: Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity (hearing loss), severe nausea. 🌟
  • 2. Antimetabolites (Folate antagonists):
    • Methotrexate: Blocks dihydrofolate reductase. Uses: Leukemia, breast cancer, rheumatoid arthritis. AE: Hepatotoxicity, myelosuppression. Give Leucovorin (folinic acid) to "rescue" normal cells from toxicity. 🌟
    • 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU): Blocks thymidylate synthase. Uses: Colorectal cancer.
  • 3. Anthracyclines (DNA intercalators):
    • Doxorubicin: Generates free radicals that damage DNA. AE: Cardiotoxicity (heart failure). Maximum lifetime dose: 450 mg/m² to prevent irreversible heart damage. 🌟
    • Daunorubicin: Used for acute leukemias. (Same cardiotoxicity risk).
  • 4. Monoclonal Antibodies (Targeted therapy):
    • Trastuzumab (Herceptin): Targets HER2 receptor. Used for HER2+ breast cancer. AE: Cardiotoxicity (monitor echocardiograms). 🌟
    • Rituximab: Targets CD20 on B-cells. Used for lymphomas and autoimmune diseases (RA).
  • ⚠️ Anthracyclines (Doxorubicin): Lifetime dose must not exceed 450 mg/m² to avoid irreversible, fatal heart failure. Monitor LVEF (left ventricular ejection fraction) closely.

  • 🧠 Mnemonic: "Chemo Side Effects – HGB"

    Hair loss, Gut issues (mucositis), Bone marrow suppression (low WBC/Platelets).
